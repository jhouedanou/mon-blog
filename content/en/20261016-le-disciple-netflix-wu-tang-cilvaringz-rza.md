First, an important disclaimer.

**This is not a film review.**

I'm not becoming a film critic. No score out of 5, no stars, no "bold direction". Some people around me do that very well, and I don't want them to think I'm copying them. Above all, I don't want a four-minute WhatsApp voice note tonight.

So no. I just watched a documentary on Netflix, and I want to talk about it. The way you talk about it at the maquis, between two skewers. That's all.

## The film in two sentences

*The Disciple: A Wu-Tang Fable* (*Le Disciple : Dans les pas du Wu-Tang Clan* in French) came out on Netflix on October 16, 2026. It's a 1 h 45 documentary directed by Joanna Natasegara, a producer who already has an Oscar at home.

It tells the story of Cilvaringz, a Dutch-Moroccan fan who made his way into the Wu-Tang Clan's inner circle. And the story of the most expensive album of all time.

## A fun watch, and that's already good

My verdict (which is not a review, we agree on that): it's a fun watch. You have a good time. You put it on on a Friday night, and you forget about your phone.

But let's be honest: this film won't make me listen to the Wu-Tang again. My old albums won't come out of the cupboard tomorrow morning. That's not the point, and that's fine.

What I liked was something else: seeing something positive. A kid from the projects who becomes a boss. A boss who reaches out. A fan who becomes a colleague. In 2026, that feels good.

## The Wu-Tang, my therapy

And yet, the Wu-Tang matters to me. When I was growing up, this group was a kind of therapy.

The funny thing is, I didn't like rap. What drew me in was the mix: kung fu and rap. Clips from old kung fu movies between the tracks, and dark beats. I had never heard anything like it. The group's name actually comes from a kung fu film, *Shaolin and Wu Tang*, released in 1983.

And next to the negativity of rap back then, the Wu-Tang was clearly above the rest. At least until *Wu-Tang Forever*, in 1997. After that, the fall was hard.

## RZA: from instant noodles to a silver box

Before we talk about the disciple, let's talk about the master.

RZA, real name Robert Fitzgerald Diggs, was born in 1969 in Brownsville, Brooklyn. He grew up partly in Staten Island, in the Stapleton and Park Hill projects. According to his younger brother, the rapper 9th Prince, they lived in a small one-bedroom apartment. Sometimes six or seven people slept there. No breakfast. Instant noodles. "Times was definitely bad back then," his brother sums up.

In short, a long way from a silver box.

And the early days were no fairy tale:

- In 1989, the Tommy Boy label signed him as Prince Rakeem. The single flopped. The label dropped him.
- He fell into petty crime. He was charged with attempted murder in Ohio, then acquitted. He himself calls it a "second chance".

He didn't waste that second chance. In 1992, he founded the Wu-Tang Clan with his cousins GZA and Ol' Dirty Bastard, and friends from the neighborhood. He produced the group's tracks, then the first solo albums of its members.

And he signed a very smart deal with Loud Records: the group was signed together, but each member could release solo albums on any label they wanted. "Wu Tang was a financial movement," he later said.

![RZA, in a black cap and glasses, at the microphone during a panel at San Diego Comic-Con in 2018](/images/articles/rza-comic-con-2018.webp)

What came next? Classics, solo albums by the dozen, then cinema. The score for Jim Jarmusch's *Ghost Dog*. The music for Tarantino's *Kill Bill*. And even a film he directed himself, *The Man with the Iron Fists*, in 2012.

From one bedroom for seven people to Hollywood. Ekié.

## Cilvaringz, or the art of never letting go

Now, the disciple.

Tarik Azzougarh, aka Cilvaringz, is Dutch-Moroccan. As a teenager in Tilburg, in the Netherlands, he discovered the Wu-Tang while playing basketball with his friends.

On May 26, 1997, the Wu-Tang played in Amsterdam. Cilvaringz wanted to get on stage, but the crowd was too big. Ol' Dirty Bastard saw him struggling, pulled him up on stage and handed him his mic. Cilvaringz freestyled. The whole venue listened. Afterwards, Method Man and ODB took him aside and introduced him to RZA.

Then the concert ended. And he lost contact.

Anyone else would have told the story to their grandchildren and moved on. Not him. He flew to New York. Several times. Just to find RZA.

Imagine doing that today: taking an Abidjan–New York flight to track down a producer who doesn't answer your messages. Your family would organize a prayer vigil.

And yet, it worked. In 1999, RZA signed him to Wu-Tang Records. Cilvaringz became the first non-American member of the Wu-Tang family.

That's what I liked most in the film. RZA could have ignored this Dutch kid. He gave him his chance. Getting out of the projects is good. Leaving the door open behind you is better.

![Cilvaringz today, smiling, in a white hoodie, black and white photo](/images/articles/the-disciple-cilvaringz.webp)

## The most expensive album of all time

And this disciple returned the favor with a completely crazy idea.

In the late 2000s, Cilvaringz came up with a Wu-Tang album in **a single copy**. No streaming. No CD in stores. No vinyl. One record only, like a painting in a museum. The idea: protest against music that had become free and disposable. RZA followed.

The result is called *Once Upon a Time in Shaolin*. 31 tracks, recorded in secret over about six years, stored in a handmade silver box. With one rule: it cannot be sold to the public before 2103. Yes, 2103. Take care of yourselves.

In 2015, the album was sold at auction. The buyer: Martin Shkreli, a highly controversial businessman. The price: 2 million dollars, about 1.2 billion CFA francs. Guinness World Records named it the most valuable album in the world, ahead of records by Elvis and the Beatles.

What followed is worthy of a movie. Shkreli was convicted of fraud. In 2018, the US justice system seized his assets, album included. In 2021, the government sold it. Then an NFT collectors' collective, PleasrDAO, bought it for 4 million dollars, about 2.3 billion CFA francs.

An album that went from the studio to the courtroom, then to crypto. As I wrote [about Bill Gates](/en/20260907-bill-gates-ia-hypocrisie-meta-cash-rules-everything): Cash Rules Everything Around Me. The Wu-Tang called it in 1993.

A small detail, to be complete: the nine members of the Wu-Tang only appear in archive footage. No new interviews. For a film about the Wu-Tang, that's a bit of a shame. But I'm not a film critic, so I didn't say anything. (Remember: I said nothing.)

## My top 5 producers

Since we're talking about RZA, a quick aside. I [make boom bap myself on Koala Sampler](/en/20260822-koala-sampler-gratuit-mac-boom-bap), so I have opinions about producers. Here's my top 5:

1. **RZA**, obviously;
2. **DJ Premier**, aka Premo;
3. **J Dilla**;
4. **Just Blaze**;
5. **Easy Mo Bee**.

## In short

*The Disciple* is a good time. Not a revelation, not a masterpiece. But a beautiful story: a kid from the projects becomes a boss, and gives his chance to a fan from the Netherlands. Together, they create a one-of-a-kind album, sold for a fortune, seized by the courts and bought back by crypto fans.

I won't be listening to the Wu-Tang on repeat this weekend. But I smiled from start to finish.

And this documentary inspires me. It makes me want to avoid certain clichés, and do what makes sense.

And I'll say it again, for the people concerned: this was not a film review.
