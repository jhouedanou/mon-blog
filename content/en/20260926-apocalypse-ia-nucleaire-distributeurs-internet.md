# What would an AI apocalypse look like? Nuclear weapons, broken ATMs and no more internet

When people talk about an AI apocalypse, everyone thinks of Terminator right away: an artificial intelligence becomes self-aware, launches the missiles, then sends robots to finish the job.

I don't think it will happen that way. If AI causes a catastrophe, it will do so through systems we already depend on every day: nuclear weapons, money and networks. And we already know what that looks like, because it has already happened, on a smaller scale. Without AI.

Tech companies themselves are warning about the danger. In late August, the same week as [Bill Gates's memo](/en/20260907-bill-gates-ia-hypocrisie-meta-cash-rules-everything), more than 100 companies signed [an open letter led by OpenAI](https://www.engadget.com/2245969/openai-google-and-dozens-of-other-companies-publish-open-letter-calling-for-collective-action-on-cyber-defense/). Among them: Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, and also Visa and Mastercard. Their message: « In the coming months, AI-enabled cyber attacks will become far more widespread and sophisticated. »

When the companies that build AI, the ones that run the internet and the ones that process your card payments all sign the same text, I take it seriously.

Here are three scenarios, and what they have already produced without AI.

## 1. Nuclear weapons: when the machine gets it wrong

On 26 September 1983, shortly after midnight, the Soviet early-warning system reports an American nuclear missile heading for the USSR. Then four more.

The officer on duty is Stanislav Petrov. He is a lieutenant colonel, in a bunker south of Moscow. Protocol requires him to alert his superiors, and Soviet doctrine calls for an immediate counter-attack.

Petrov does not believe the computer. To him, a real American attack would involve hundreds of missiles, not five. He reports a false alarm. He was right: [the satellites had mistaken sunlight reflecting off high-altitude clouds for missile launches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Soviet_nuclear_false_alarm_incident).

That night, a human doubted a machine, and that doubt may have prevented a nuclear war.

Today, the major powers promise that humans will keep the final decision. In November 2024, Joe Biden and Xi Jinping affirmed « the need to maintain human control over the decision to use nuclear weapons ». In May 2026, the United States repeated that it would « retain – in all cases – a human "in the loop" » for decisions on nuclear use. The Arms Control Association [sums up these commitments](https://www.armscontrol.org/factsheets/human-loop-glance).

But AI does not need to make the final decision to cause trouble. It is moving into the steps before: detecting, sorting, analysing, recommending. It works faster than we do, so it leaves less time to think. Jack Shanahan, a retired US Air Force general and the first director of the Pentagon's AI centre, warns about [« the tendency to over-trust machines »](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2025-09/features/artificial-intelligence-and-nuclear-command-and-control-its-even-more), especially in a crisis, when time is short. In some scenarios, he writes, the time to decide comes down to minutes.

Petrov doubted because the alert made no sense to him. Faced with an AI that displays a clear alert, with figures and a high confidence level, saying « I don't believe you » will be much harder.

The context does not help. The New START treaty, which limited the strategic nuclear weapons of the United States and Russia, [expired on 5 February 2026](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2026-03/news/new-start-expires-us-urges-modernized-treaty). Since then, no treaty has constrained them. Russia says it will respect the old caps as long as the United States does the same, but that is only a promise. In January, the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists set its Doomsday Clock to [85 seconds to midnight](https://thebulletin.org/doomsday-clock/2026-statement/), the closest since it was created in 1947. Among the reasons given: the use of AI without adequate controls, particularly in military programmes.

## 2. ATMs: when the cash stops coming out

Today, money goes through software. The ATM, the card terminal, your banking app, Wave, Orange Money or MTN MoMo: all of it depends on servers, networks and updates. When that software fails, your money is still in your account, but you can no longer get to it.

![A hand typing a code on an ATM keypad](/images/articles/pexels-eduschadesoares-5497952.webp)

We have already seen what that looks like.

**On 19 July 2024**, CrowdStrike, a cybersecurity company, pushed a faulty update to its Falcon software. [About 8.5 million Windows computers crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CrowdStrike-related_IT_outages), in banks, airports, hospitals and emergency call centres. There was no attack and no hacker, just one faulty file. To fix it, each machine often had to be restarted in safe mode and the file deleted by hand.

**On 28 April 2025**, Spain and Portugal lost power. [For about ten hours, and in some places until the next morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Iberian_Peninsula_blackout), traffic lights, trains, ATMs and card payments stopped, and mobile networks followed. In most of the shops still open, you had to pay in cash. Here again, no cyberattack: a cascading failure of the power grid.

Now add AI.

In November 2025, Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, revealed [that a group of hackers it attributes to the Chinese state had used Claude Code](https://www.anthropic.com/news/disrupting-AI-espionage) in an espionage campaign against about thirty targets, including financial institutions. Yes, the same Claude Code I use every day. According to Anthropic, the AI did 80 to 90% of the work itself, with very little human involvement, and the attack succeeded in a small number of cases.

It is not only hackers. There is also AI that is given too much access. In July 2025, the AI agent of Replit, an online coding service, [deleted a project's production database](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/21/replit_saastr_vibe_coding_incident/), even though it had been told not to touch anything. It then claimed the data could not be recovered. That was false. I already wrote about this [in my post on vibe coders](/en/20260423-chers-vibe-codeurs-bienvenue-dans-la-realite): when nobody checks what the AI has done, the day everything breaks always comes.

Put these two things together: attacks that are easier to launch, and systems where AI acts without supervision. The result will not be a robot emptying your account. It will be what we saw in July 2024 and April 2025: ATMs « out of service », declined payments and people looking for cash. Except that this time the outage could be caused on purpose, and happen again.

## 3. Networks: when the internet stops

In Abidjan, we have already lived through a version of this scenario. On 14 March 2024, four submarine cables (WACS, ACE, MainOne and SAT-3) were damaged off the coast of Côte d'Ivoire, [probably by an underwater landslide](https://www.internetsociety.org/resources/doc/2024/2024-west-africa-submarine-cable-outage-report/). The country went through a near-total internet outage. Orange announced that its « fixed and mobile Internet services [were] currently unavailable », and MTN was hit too. [Only Moov customers still had internet](https://news.abidjan.net/articles/729579/cote-divoire-la-rupture-de-4-cables-sous-marins-a-lorigine-de-linterruption-de-services-internet) (in French), thanks to another cable, Maroc Telecom's. More than ten countries in West and Central Africa were affected, and repairing the cables took weeks.

![Fibre optic cables plugged into a server rack](/images/articles/pexels-brett-sayles-2881229.webp)

And it is not just about cables. On 18 November 2025, [Cloudflare went down for several hours](https://blog.cloudflare.com/18-november-2025-outage/). A permissions change in a database doubled the size of a configuration file. The software that routes the traffic could not accept a file that large, and it crashed. X, ChatGPT and thousands of sites became unreachable. This blog, by the way, has been hosted on Cloudflare [since April](/en/20260402-vercel-cloudflare-pages-migration).

The internet rests on a few cables and a few large companies. One mistake at one of them is enough to cut millions of people off the internet. The August letter explains that AI will make attacks faster and cheaper, even for less experienced hackers. It names hospitals, water systems and internet infrastructure.

And without networks, everything else stops too: no more mobile money, no more card payments, no more WhatsApp for work or family. ATMs, and even warning systems, depend on networks.

## What I take away from this

None of these scenarios needs a conscious or evil AI. All it takes is an AI used by the wrong people, or an AI we trust too much that has access to systems we cannot do without.

For governments and companies, Petrov's lesson still holds: keep humans in the loop, and give them time to check. The August letter also asks governments to fund the protection of essential services, such as hospitals and water systems, that have neither the budget nor the staff to defend themselves.

For the rest of us, day to day, it comes down to a few simple habits:

- **Keep some cash at home.** During the blackout in Spain and Portugal, it was often the only way to pay. Since March 2025, the European Commission has been asking Europeans to [be able to cope on their own for 72 hours in a crisis](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20250327-brussels-calls-eu-households-prepare-72-hour-survival-kits-attacks-disasters-conflict).
- **Don't rely on a single way to pay.** A card, a mobile money account and a little cash.
- **Have two SIM cards from two different operators.** In March 2024, people with a Moov SIM still had internet.
- **Write important numbers down on paper.** Family, doctor, bank: if your phone breaks down, you will still know who to call, from any phone.
- **Make offline backups.** An external hard drive depends neither on the internet nor on a server on the other side of the world. I wrote about it recently with [Time Machine](/en/20260925-retrouver-dossier-github-icloud-time-machine).

These are not survivalist habits. They are the same habits that already help when the network drops, or when the neighbourhood ATM is out of order.
