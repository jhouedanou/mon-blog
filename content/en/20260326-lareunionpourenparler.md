In my line of work, there is a very common disease, the useless meeting.

I don't mean the "crisis meeting" or the "kickoff".

That one only shows up when ego and lack of arguments, carefully cultivated by an education system that rewards regurgitation, run into [technical reality](/en/20260707-je-suis-encore-vivant).

## The "let's talk about it" meeting

It always starts the same way, with a request made with the nonchalance of someone who won't have to carry it out.
It gets [dropped between two messages](/en/20261028-whatsapp-nest-pas-un-moyen-de-communication-professionnel), as if the effort in time and resources it represents did not exist. It is a big request, made with full confidence.

In response, you send a technical analysis: options compared, risks anticipated, recommendations for tools that already do the job, a detailed development plan. So you do the thinking for the person who didn't take the time to do it.

The reply is immediate and vague.
*"I don't really see it. I'm not comfortable with this."*

Followed by the ritual proposal:
*"Can we do a call to talk about it?"*
Which, translated, means:

*"I have no counter-argument, I only have a conviction."*

The problem runs deeper than a colleague's ego or a company's culture, it is structural.

Our education system, in French-speaking Africa, in France, or anywhere else in the sphere inherited from the French model, does not train people for adversarial thinking and pragmatism, unlike the Anglo-Saxon world. It trains them to regurgitate.

You learn to recite, to validate, to tick boxes, and the person who gives the right answer gets rewarded rather than the one who asks the best question. Instead of developing debate as a skill, you learn to see it as a threat and to dodge it.

The useless meeting is a consequence of this lack of training.

- Nobody learned how to formulate a rigorous objection in front of their teachers.
- Nobody learned to tell an intuition from an argument.

So a meeting gets called, because the meeting, at least, gives the illusion of seriousness without requiring any of its substance.

## How do you avoid a useless meeting?

At that moment, you have to avoid accepting yet another meeting that will lead nowhere, because you have already proposed a reasoned and documented solution. Nothing productive will come out of that meeting, I guarantee it.
Be pragmatic, and ask the other person to [put their concerns in writing](/en/20260908-cest-integre-dans-la-maquette). That is a healthy basis for continuing the conversation.

## And did it work in your case?

Yes, but only by insisting… In many (too many) cases, I got one of these replies:

"We'll see."\
"It's not that urgent."\
"I'm busy right now."

In those cases, keep insisting, not to humiliate anyone, but to help the person articulate what is bothering them and turn vague ideas into real objections. And if, despite your efforts, the written answer never comes (or comes back empty), then no meeting, because a useful meeting starts with a useful sentence.

PS: If you decide to hold that meeting anyway, plan an agenda, a time limit (10 minutes max, no lateness tolerated) and a single question to settle, with arguments.
