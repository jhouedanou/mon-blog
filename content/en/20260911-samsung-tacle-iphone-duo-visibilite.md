# Samsung mocks the iPhone Duo, and that's very good for Samsung

I followed this week's Apple event with half an eye, mainly to see the iPhone Duo I told you about in [my previous post](/en/20260911-site-responsive-iphone-duo). But the most entertaining part wasn't happening on the Cupertino stage. It was happening on X, on the Samsung Mobile US account, which spent the entire presentation taking live shots at Apple.


## The jabs

The account published half a dozen messages in an hour, all on the same theme: Apple copies. « So far, so same », « It's giving groundhog day », and above all « Let us know when you're done reheating our leftovers », a pointed nod to the fact that Apple is arriving on the foldables market seven years after the first Galaxy Fold. TV5 Monde [summed up the episode](https://information.tv5monde.com/international/ils-nous-ont-copies-samsung-tacle-le-nouvel-iphone-pliable-dapple-sur-les-reseaux-sociaux-2836914) for those who weren't in front of their screens.

![Screenshot of the Samsung Mobile US tweets published during the Apple keynote](/images/articles/yeahno.jpeg)

Granted, it's fair game, and Samsung has a long habit of this kind of exercise. We remember the ads mocking the absence of a headphone jack on the iPhone, before Samsung removed it too. On the substance, the Korean manufacturer isn't wrong: it has been selling foldable phones since 2019 and is on its eighth generation. Apple shows up with a first model, and that first model looks a lot like a Galaxy Z Fold.

## The lookalike

The tweets were just the warm-up. Samsung also released a video in which a white-haired man, thin glasses and black shirt, poses on a stool with a folded Galaxy in hand, in front of his name written in giant letters: Tim Cook. Except it's not Apple's boss, it's a lookalike who, we're told, really is called Tim Cook. What a coincidence 🙂

![The Tim Cook lookalike, sitting on a stool with a folded Galaxy in hand, in front of his name written in large letters](/images/articles/tim-cook-temu.png)

## Who benefits from the joke?

This is where I paused for a moment. Look at the numbers under the tweets: 612,000 views for the first one, over 100,000 for most of the others. For a brand account on the night of an Apple keynote, that's enormous, and all it cost them was a community manager and a good dose of bad faith.

The whole tech planet was watching Apple that night. Samsung had nothing to announce, no reason to be part of the conversation. By hitching itself to the wagon, it grabbed part of the attention that was going entirely to its competitor. Every article that talks about « reheated leftovers » puts Samsung in its headline, including this one. Deep down, those tweets aren't addressed to Apple, they're addressed to us, and their only purpose is to scrape a bit of visibility off the back of the event of the year.

What's more, by repeating that Apple copies the Galaxy Fold, Samsung reminds everyone that the Galaxy Fold exists, that it has been around for a long time, and that the foldable format is no longer a fragile gadget reserved for the curious. The Galaxy Z Fold 8 has in fact found a good compromise between the flip model and the book format: less bulky, lighter, more manageable day to day. So Samsung has an ideal window to consolidate its leadership in this market while Apple takes its first steps.

## The detail that slightly spoils the joke

There is one point the tweets forget to mention, though. The iPhone Duo's foldable OLED screen, the most expensive and most complicated part of the phone, is made by Samsung Display. In other words, every iPhone Duo sold puts money into the South Korean group's coffers, as [Phonandroid](https://www.phonandroid.com/pourquoi-chaque-iphone-duo-vendu-par-apple-fait-la-fortune-de-samsung.html) explains.

So Samsung Mobile mocks a product whose every sale delights Samsung Display. Both entities belong to the same group, and I wouldn't be surprised if, in Seoul, the situation is seen as rather comfortable: if the iPhone Duo is a failure, the Galaxy Fold remains the king of foldables; if it's a success, Samsung still pockets its share on every screen.

## And what about the iPhone Duo in all this?

To be honest, I don't have an opinion on the phone itself yet. It's a first model, it's expensive, and it does look a lot like what Samsung has been doing for several years. But we know the Apple method: arrive late, with a product that invents nothing, then convince the general public that the category exists. Smartwatches and wireless earbuds existed well before the Apple Watch and AirPods.

If that happens with foldables, Samsung will have advertised the category while thinking it was mocking Apple. And it will sell the screens. Clearly, it's not the one with the most to lose in this story 🙂
