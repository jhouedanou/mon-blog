There are three trades whose members, many of them anyway, will not be going to heaven: mechanics, carpenters and electronics salespeople (and by extension, their TikTok variants).

The first because of their tendency to overcharge for repairs, the second because of their notorious inability to respect deadlines and appointments, and the last because a good number of them do not know much about anything, but fill their intellectual void with arrogance or dishonesty.

Take for example this salesman I met today in a big store in town, while I was looking for a cable to charge my (not that old, actually) 2017 MacBook Pro.

"Hello sir, can I help you?"

"Hello, I'm looking for a cable for my 2017 MacBook Pro. USB-C, please."

Ten seconds later, the salesman hands me a 240W USB-C cable.

"No, 60W is enough for my 2017 MacBook Pro."

"Ahh, boss! You need to take that 240W cable there. That's the good one, dêh!" he insists, waving a cable under my nose.

I take a deep breath, trying to stay calm despite my growing impatience.

"Listen, I know what I need. 240W is overkill for my model."

"No deh! This is the best cable oh. It's going to charge the computer zoo!" he persists, a salesman's smile on his lips.

I reply firmly: "I assure you it won't. My 2017 MacBook Pro uses an 87W charger at most. The 240W cable will not charge it faster. In fact, a simple 60W cable would be more than enough."

The salesman frowns, visibly annoyed to see his sale slipping away.

I shake my head and unpack my knowledge to put an end to this conversation that is dragging on:

"My MacBook is not designed to take a 240W charge. It uses a less powerful charging standard. The charging speed is limited by the computer's internal controller, not by the cable. Besides, the MacBook automatically negotiates the optimal power with the charger."

The salesman stares at me, mouth open.

I go on: "To get the fastest possible charge, I just need a charger with the same power as the original. A more powerful cable brings no advantage whatsoever."

Thrown off, the salesman tries one last approach: "But... but come on, usually, well... that's what we sell here ohh."

I reply, finally spotting the 60W cable I need: "Not in this case. This one will do perfectly. Thanks for your help, but I'll take this one."

I grab the 60W cable, leaving the salesman puzzled, and head to the till, satisfied to have avoided being oversold and eager to finally leave with the right product.

More seriously... your 2017 MacBook Pro is not a race car built to swallow 240W like a light breakfast. It prefers a more modest diet, with a less powerful charging standard. Yes, the 240W cable is backward compatible, like an adult who knows how to talk to children. But it will not force your MacBook to eat more than it can.

**Remember that in the world of technology, as in life, it is not always the biggest or the most powerful that wins. It is the one best suited to your needs.**

And that, no arrogant salesman can sell you: it is up to you to know it.
