Google's Find Hub located my lost phone in Abobo. An honest lady did the rest.

It all starts with a broken electric window.

I had left the car at the office to sort out that little problem of a window that wouldn't go back up, which can turn out to be a real nuisance in this rainy season.

With no vehicle of my own, I was getting around by Yango. And there came the first mistake, a silly one, but it's what started this whole story, I got in the back. That's not how we do things around here, because normally I sit in the front, but that evening I wanted a bit of comfort, which I regretted afterwards.

The day then goes on as usual ([walls to climb over](/en/20260707-je-suis-encore-vivant), bugs fixed and emergencies handled). Tired, I pick up my car around 4 pm and set off for home around 9 pm (I live in Riviera Triangle, and that's the only time that lets me leave the office and get home in under 30 minutes with no traffic jams).

Once I get near the Lycée Français, I realise that one of my phones, a CMF Phone 1 I use for mobile development, is still in the Yango.

Out of developer reflex, I grab another phone and open **[Find Hub](https://www.google.com/android/find)** (Google's new "Find My Device"). And there's the blue dot, sitting quietly... in Abobo, behind the new CHU. For once, location tracking is working for me, while usually, on this blog, it's more [the AirTag in the company car telling the boss everything](/en/20250705-cherscommerciaux).

## The Abobo aside (because it has to be said)

I know exactly what you're thinking, and I'm going to stop you right there.

No, I'm not going to trot out the clichés about Abobo, the neighbourhood, its residents, because not everyone in Abobo is what Abidjan gossip likes to say. Keep that in mind, the end of the story will confirm it better than any speech.

## The longest night

Now for the panic, which is less about the phone than about what's inside it:

- **Authenticator**, so my two-factor logins.
- **Ecobank, [Djamo](/en/20240905-djamo-adieu-frais-de-rejet-bonjour-transferts-faciles-et-solde-discret) & Wave**, so the money.
- And above all **ALL the notifications**, including the bank's.

And on the way home, the car throws up a big STOP related to an ABS cable failure, so a serious breakdown. Thank you, life.

## The next day, I was already grieving

By morning, I had filed the matter under "lost". I had moved on to grief, the acceptance stage. And that's exactly when the other phone rings, and it's Mr Houédanou Senior, my father.

> — Hello
> — Hello
> — How are you?
> — Meh.
> — Did you lose your phone?
> — Er... yes.
> — A lady called me this morning to tell me she found it in a Yango.
> — Ok, Dad. Give me her number, I'll call her.

I should say that on all my phones, the lock screen message reads: "Please contact me at..." (the number of Casa Houédanou). So my father gets told, and he calls me.

The system proved itself the time I lost another phone, so I'm keeping it, because it's effective and cheap.

## The logistics (and the bill)

I'm in Riviera Triangle and the phone is in Abobo, with all of Abidjan between the two.

- Cost of the trip: **5,600 FCFA**.
- There **and** back: **11,200 FCFA**.

Find Hub put the dot "within two streets". Except that two streets, in Abobo, matter. After asking around and making a phone call, it wasn't exactly there, and I had to head for the Container junction.

## The golden lady

And here's the ending I promised you. A very kind lady hands me back the phone, with no fuss, no haggling, no "so what about my cut".

She did insist that I unlock the phone to check it really was mine. I did, and she gave me my phone back. I returned the favour, of course, even though honesty can't be repaid.

**So much for Abobo and your two-bit clichés.**

## What I take away from the lost phone and Find Hub

Three lessons, free of charge, learned for 11,200 FCFA:

1. **[Find Hub](https://www.google.com/android/find) is good, but it's not precise.** It puts you in the right neighbourhood, but you still have to find the right door. Google says so itself: GPS places the device to within about 20 metres, the mobile network to within a few thousand metres, and the circle around the pin shows how reliable the position is ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/6160491?hl=en){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}). Be ready to finish the investigation the old-fashioned way, by phone and on foot.
2. **Don't act like the boss, don't sit in the back of the Yango.** Even if it's an Alto that comes to pick you up. In the front, you see your things, you see your pocket and you get out in one go, while in the back, you leave things behind.
3. **Yango drivers aren't always pros.** Some don't even check the back seat after the ride. The "get out properly" reflex has to be yours.
