What I just watched is probably the craziest product demo of the year.

No, it's not AI.

This car flies.

Yes. It flies. I saw it take off, turn in the air and land. And I'm still not over it.

## Nine years of waiting

Quick reminder. Tesla announced the new Roadster in November 2017. Deliveries were promised for 2020. Then nothing. Delays, more delays, and unveiling dates pushed back one after another.

And on Thursday, October 15, 2026, in Texas, near Waco, Tesla finally showed it. Nine years later.

Elon Musk had talked about "the most exciting, whether it works or not, demo ever of any product". Spoiler: it worked.

## Yes, it flies (I'll say it again)

![The new Tesla Roadster in the air, in a cloud of smoke, next to the demo ramp](/images/articles/tesla-roadster-en-vol.webp)

OK, not like a plane. It has no wings. It won't fly you from Abidjan to Yamoussoukro. But it takes off, it spins and it lands. For a car, that's not bad at all.

Here's what it did during the demo:

- It drove up a ramp until it was **upside down**. Fans suck the air from under the car and hold it against the ramp.
- It launched into the air, spun around, and landed gently.
- It jumped over **five Tesla vehicles**: a Cybercab, a Cybertruck, a Semi, a Model Y and a Model 3.

The secret? SpaceX thrusters. They are cold-gas thrusters: they blow out compressed air. Pssshhh. On the wildest version, there are ten of them all around the car. They are connected to 600 liters of compressed air, according to Lars Moravy, Tesla's vice president of engineering. The more air, the longer the car stays in the air. And the tanks can be refilled.

Picture this on the HKB bridge in Abidjan on a Monday morning. Traffic jam? Pssshhh. You fly over the minibuses. (No, it's not allowed. But let me dream.)

## It defies the laws of physics (well, almost)

During the event, Tesla said this car "breaks the laws of physics". It doesn't really break them. It cheats. And it cheats very well.

**First magic trick: suction.** Under the car, big fans suck out the air. That creates a low-pressure area under the car. The result: the car is pressed against the road. Hard enough to drive upside down, as we saw on the ramp.

The system has two modes:

- **at low speed**, it sucks as hard as it can, for maximum grip when starting;
- **at high speed**, it opens passages at the front and back, to let air flow better under the car.

And in corners, it changes everything. According to Tesla, tires alone hold about 1.26 G. With suction, the Roadster takes corners at more than 2 G. Put simply: in the corner, you're pushed sideways with a force equal to twice your weight. Your lunch too.

**Second magic trick: the thrusters.** At the back, they add their thrust to the motors'. Tesla claims more than 80 kilonewtons. That's roughly the force needed to lift 8 tonnes. In a sports car. Yes.

These thrusters also make the stunts in the air possible. They handle the car's balance during the jump. Tesla calls it "gymnastics". I call it witchcraft.

**And inside?** Two seats, a very simple interior, a look inspired by aircraft. The steering wheel isn't mechanically connected to the wheels: everything goes through electronics. That's called "steer-by-wire".

## Three versions, from "fast" to "rocket"

Tesla offers three versions. The last two add thrusters to the first one.

- **Performance**, the road car. Two seats, moving wings, and the suction system. 0 to 60 mph in under 1.6 seconds.
- **Thrust**, for the track. Two seats, with thrusters at the back. 0 to 60 mph in under 0.9 seconds.
- **Rocket**, the one that flies. One seat, ten thrusters all around. 0 to 60 mph in under 0.59 seconds. Under 0.59 seconds! You blink, and you're already at 60 mph.

Small detail: the Rocket is invite only. And according to Tesla, it isn't approved for public roads.

Tesla also claims more than 300 miles of range and a top speed of more than 230 mph. All these numbers come from Tesla. Nobody has checked them yet.

![A driver in a white suit, helmet in hand, walks toward the new white Tesla Roadster parked in a desert](/images/articles/tesla-roadster-desert.webp)

## So, what about the price?

Mystery. Tesla gave no price. For any version.

What we know are the reservations:

- **Performance**: $50,000, about 29 million CFA francs. You pay $5,000 right away, then $45,000 within ten days. Otherwise, goodbye reservation.
- **Thrust**: $250,000, about 146 million CFA francs.
- **Rocket**: invite only. No price. If you have to ask, it's not for you.

In 2017, Tesla announced $200,000 for the base version. And $250,000 for the "Founders" series, the first 1,000 cars. Nothing says the price will be the same today.

And production? 2028, according to Musk. After nine years of delays, I wouldn't bet my car on it. But I want to believe.

## What changed since 2017

The 2026 Roadster isn't the one promised in 2017. Back then, Tesla announced 620 miles of range, four seats and more than 250 mph. Today: more than 300 miles, two seats at most, and more than 230 mph.

The Roadster lost range and seats. But it gained thrusters. Honestly? Good trade.

## Safety? Not really the point

Musk doesn't pretend. Before the event, he said on a podcast: "If safety is your number one goal, do not buy the Roadster." He also said Tesla would "aspire not to kill anyone in this car".

Reassuring. Well, almost.

Another detail: the compressed air is stored in carbon fiber tanks. It's the same type of tank as those linked to several SpaceX rocket explosions. We've heard more soothing sales pitches.

## My take

I'm not the target audience. With 29 million CFA francs just to reserve one, very few people are.

But I admit it: I watched the demo like a kid. A car driving upside down. Jumping over five others. Landing like a SpaceX rocket. Nine years of delays, no price, promises scaled back… and yet, I smiled from start to finish.

Because this car flies. And nobody can take that away from it.

See you in 2028. Maybe.
