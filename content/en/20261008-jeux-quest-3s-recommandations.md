People often ask me which games to install on a Meta Quest 3S, so here's my list. I'm not claiming these are the best games in the world according to the press, they're the ones I play, in my living room, in Abidjan, sweating.

I've ranked them from easiest to hardest, and for each one you get what it's about, its personality and my take. At the very bottom sits Vendetta Forever, and it's not there by accident.

## Easy, no reflexes needed

### République VR

![Hope, hood up, in République VR](/images/articles/quest3s-republique.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/republique-vr/1794123900713107/)

**The story**: Hope is a young woman locked inside Metamorphosis, the [closed facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/République_%28video_game%29) of a totalitarian state, run by a man known as the Overseer, where everyone is watched. You don't play Hope, you hack the surveillance system and you guide her through the cameras to help her escape.

The game comes from Camouflaj, a Seattle studio whose founder, Ryan Payton, worked on *Metal Gear Solid 4*. It was born from a Kickstarter campaign in 2012, it came out in episodes on iPhone from 2013, and the virtual reality version arrived on Quest in [July 2019](https://uploadvr.com/republique-oculus-quest/).

**Its personality**: the spy novel of the bunch, calm, patient, a little paranoid, the one you launch with a cup of tea rather than a towel around your neck.

**My take**: it may be my favourite on the list, because it's the only one with a story, with characters, stealth and suspense. And playing the one watching through the cameras works even better in virtual reality, since you're in the surveillance room yourself. No need to sweat or to have reflexes, you just have to think, and want to know what happens next.

### Vader Immortal

![A blue lightsaber facing a TIE fighter in Vader Immortal](/images/articles/quest3s-vader-immortal.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/vader-immortal-series/682579266195262/)

**The story**: you're a smuggler captured by Darth Vader on Mustafar, his lava planet, and you discover you can use the Force and a lightsaber. The series comes from ILMxLAB, Lucasfilm's studio, and has three episodes, released in 2019.

**Its personality**: the luxury blockbuster, beautiful, impressive, generous with the thrills, and over too soon.

**My take**: very good. You hold a lightsaber, you stand face to face with Vader and you hear him breathing right next to you, and this is exactly what virtual reality does well.

The Force is well implemented, you reach out to pull an object or throw an enemy, and it works, you feel like a Jedi. The game guides you from start to finish and never leaves you stuck.

The problem is the length, because the three episodes run [about two hours](https://www.engadget.com/2019/10/31/vader-immortal-episode-3-star-wars) in total, the length of a film. You want more, and there isn't any.

Luckily, there's still the "dojo" mode, which is more interesting than it sounds. You take on waves of training droids with your lightsaber, each wave harder than the last, and it can get very intense. The story is easy, the dojo much less so.

### Deadpool VR

![Deadpool's hands holding a sword and a gun in Deadpool VR](/images/articles/quest3s-deadpool.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/marvels-deadpool-vr/3240283705987790/)

**The story**: Deadpool is hired to capture villains from the Marvel universe, and it's all filmed like a TV show. The game comes from Twisted Pixel and it came out on [18 November 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel%27s_Deadpool_VR), only on Quest 3 and Quest 3S. It's the last game from the studio, which Meta shut down in January 2026.

**Its personality**: the annoying friend you love anyway. He talks too much, he makes terrible jokes, he makes fun of you, and you laugh regardless.

**My take**: it's my guilty pleasure. Deadpool never stops talking and makes fun of you and of the game itself. The fights are smooth and the difficulty is adjustable, so you can finish it without being an ace.

And there's a cameo from my favourite superhero of all time, Mister Johnny Blaze, the Ghost Rider himself ([Pocket Tactics' review confirms it](https://www.pockettactics.com/deadpool-vr-review){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}). I won't tell you where, but he deserves his own VR game, with a flaming motorbike, a chain and a burning skull. What are we waiting for?

I play in "Kidpool" mode, an option that cuts the swearing ([GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/deadpool-vr-review-merc-goes-meta){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}). Careful, though, it leaves the violence alone, and [reviews point it out](https://www.empireonline.com/gaming/reviews/deadpool-vr), you still slice enemies in half. In the United States, the game is still [rated for ages 17 and up](https://www.esrb.org/ratings/40934/marvels-deadpool-vr/). It's not a game for children, Kidpool just makes the dialogue easier to live with.

## Medium, with time in slow motion

### Superhot VR

![A red enemy shattering in Superhot VR](/images/articles/quest3s-superhot.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/superhot-vr/1921533091289407/)

**The story**: there's almost none. Red figures want to kill you, in an all-white setting.

The game used to talk to you between levels, with a little riff on control and obedience, and it went as far as asking you to shoot yourself to prove it. The studio [removed those scenes in July 2021](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/-i-superhot-vr-i-devs-remove-self-harm-elements-from-the-game-you-deserve-better-), and that's for the best.

Anyway, you're there for the hook of the game, where time only moves when you move. If you stand still, the bullets freeze in mid-air, and if you turn your head, they creep forward. The game comes from the Polish studio SUPERHOT Team, and it came out in 2016 on PC, then in 2019 on the first Quest.

**Its personality**: the Zen master. He barely says a word, he teaches you patience and he punishes you the moment you panic.

**My take**: this is the game that makes you understand virtual reality. You dodge a bullet in slow motion, you catch a gun in mid-air and you feel like you're in *The Matrix*. Since time stops, you always have time to think, and it's as much a puzzle as a shooter. A classic, the first thing to install.

### Action Hero

![Suited agents in a hotel corridor in Action Hero](/images/articles/quest3s-action-hero.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/7021123758002222/)

**The story**: you're the star of action movies. The game comes from the Swedish studio [Fast Travel Games](https://www.uploadvr.com/action-hero-quest-review/) and it came out in December 2024. It's split into films of different genres, where each film has its acts and each act its scenes.

**Its personality**: the B-movie stuntman. He overdoes it, he knows it, and that's why we love him.

**My take**: it's the same mechanic as Superhot, everything runs in slow motion and speeds up when you move. You take the time to aim, to dodge, to throw a bottle at a bad guy. But it's a notch harder than Superhot, because a single hit is enough to send you back to the start of the act. If you liked Superhot, go for it.

### Sairento VR

![An armed enemy in a neon setting in Sairento VR](/images/articles/quest3s-sairento.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/2299215130112875/)

**The story**: you're a cyber-ninja in a futuristic Japan. The game comes from Mixed Realms, a Singapore studio. The Quest version was first called *Sairento Untethered* and it came out in [June 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sairento_VR).

**Its personality**: the cousin who had too much coffee. He runs on walls, he jumps everywhere, he shouts, and he never quite knows where he's going.

**My take**: a glorious sh\*\*show. _(Sorry, Kidpool mode is still on.)_ You run on walls, you make huge jumps, you slow down time mid-air, you slice with a sword and shoot a pistol at the same time. It's great fun for an hour.

The difficulty mostly comes from the movement, you need a strong stomach. And there's no real story, the missions follow one another and nothing makes me want to know what happens next.

## Hard, you will sweat

### Ninja Legends

![A sword-wielding ninja in front of a wall of flames in Ninja Legends](/images/articles/quest3s-ninja-legends.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/ninja-legends/2398880980156491/)

**The story**: none, or nearly. Waves of assassins and warriors come at you, and you take them on with a sword, throwing weapons and special powers. The game comes from Coinflip Studios, a small studio, and it's their [first game on Quest](https://www.meta.com/blog/ninja-knowledge-vr-dev-tips-from-coinflip-studios/){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, released in 2019. The style is cartoonish, which takes the edge off the violence.

**Its personality**: the Saturday-morning cartoon, colourful, scrappy, easy-going, and more athletic than it looks.

**My take**: it's a better-behaved version of Sairento, with no story either, but easier to read, because the enemies come to you, wave after wave. The slow motion makes every sword strike very satisfying, and it's a good workout, which VR fitness sites [rank among the best](https://www.vrfitnessinsider.com/this-weeks-vr-game-roundup-ninja-legends/).

### Until You Fall

![An armoured knight surrounded by lightning in Until You Fall](/images/articles/quest3s-until-you-fall.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/until-you-fall/2567459230020142/)

**The story**: you're a rune knight and you fight in a neon-coloured fantasy world. It's a roguelite from Schell Games, released on Quest in [September 2020](https://www.roadtovr.com/until-you-fall-psvr-quest-release-date/), which means that when you die, you start again from the beginning and only keep a few upgrades.

**Its personality**: the demanding fencing teacher. He makes you repeat the same drill, again and again, until it's perfect.

**My take**: the sword fights are good. You parry, you dodge, you strike, and you feel the weight of the weapons. *Shaolin shadowboxing, and the Wu-Tang sword style*, as the intro to *Enter the Wu-Tang* would say. The difficulty is real, since dying once is enough to send you back to zero.

But it's repetitive, because a roguelite means replaying the same rooms over and over, and after a while, I get bored.

### Pistol Whip

![A giant robot in a purple neon setting in Pistol Whip](/images/articles/quest3s-pistol-whip.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/pistol-whip/2104963472963790/)

**The story**: not really one. [Cloudhead Games](https://www.roadtovr.com/pistol-whip-launch-date-quest-steamvr/) released it in November 2019, and it was billed as a mix of Superhot and Beat Saber. You move forward on a rail, to the beat of the music, you shoot everything that moves and you dodge bullets. The levels draw on action movies.

**Its personality**: the armed DJ. He never stops, and he's the one setting the tempo.

**My take**: very intense. Here, time doesn't stop and the music is in charge. There's no chance to catch your breath, because the rail keeps moving whether you're ready or not. When you shoot on the beat, you're John Wick in a music video. I launch it when I want to blow off steam for twenty minutes.

### Dragon Fist: VR Kung Fu

![A blue energy ball on a temple terrace at sunset in Dragon Fist](/images/articles/quest3s-dragon-fist.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/dragon-fist-vr-kung-fu/5075285115918784/)

**The story**: an imaginary martial arts world, where masters want to prove themselves, emperors crush anyone who threatens them and wronged peasants sharpen their blades. Basically, the plot of half the kung fu films ever made in Hong Kong.

The game comes from a small studio, Ben Olding Games, and it's published by Clique Games. It came out on Steam in late 2021 and arrived on Meta's official Store in [February 2024](https://mixed-news.com/en/dargon-fist-vr-kung-fu-quest-store-release/), with reworked graphics.

**Its personality**: the kung fu video store. It has seen everything, it knows every line, and it pulls out its favourite tapes one by one.

**My take**: it's beautiful. The settings are nicely textured, whether it's the temples, the training courtyards or the sunsets, and it sets the mood from the very first second. And the music gives it away, the main theme is clearly inspired by Wong Fei-hung's, the hero of *Once Upon a Time in China*, Tsui Hark's saga starring Jet Li.

The movement, though, isn't orthodox. You don't reproduce real techniques, you hit however you can. But all the kung fu movie archetypes are there, with barely disguised copies of Jet Li, Jackie Chan, Ip Man… and even Beatrix Kiddo, the Bride from *Kill Bill*, a blonde woman learning kung fu from an old master.

I'd have preferred to play Pai Mei, the master in question, but there's a Gordon Liu clone, the actor who played Pai Mei in *Kill Bill* and who is above all the hero of *The 36th Chamber of Shaolin*. The 36 chambers, as in *Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers)*. Yes, [the Wu-Tang again](/en/20260907-bill-gates-ia-hypocrisie-meta-cash-rules-everything). And it's fun.

The only problem is that I still can't get past the level with the three ninjas, in hard mode. Three against one, these people have no honour.

## Vendetta Forever, the hardest

![White enemies in a red and black setting in Vendetta Forever](/images/articles/quest3s-vendetta-forever.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/vendetta-forever/9177531962317371/)

**The story**: next to nothing, a string of action-movie scenes. The game comes from Meat Space Interactive, it's published by [nDreams](https://roadtovr.com/vendetta-forever-announcement-trailer-release-quest-psvr-2/) and it came out in October 2024.

You can't move freely. You kill an enemy, you grab their weapon and you get thrown to where they stood, ready to aim at the next one. ["Shoot to kill, and kill to move"](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2024/10/23/vendetta-forever-review/), as one review puts it. If there's no weapon left to grab, you're stuck.

**Its personality**: the sadistic choreographer. He has timed everything to the tenth of a second, and he's waiting for you to keep up.

**My take**: it's without a doubt the hardest game on the list. Enemies surround you and they shoot fast and straight. Each level is a little puzzle, where you have to work out in what order to kill them, and with which weapon, so you never get stuck.

You die a lot at first, then you start chaining moves without thinking: duck, shoot, grab the weapon, get thrown forward, repeat. It's even more intense than Pistol Whip, and it's a [real cardio workout](/en/20250508-project-fat-loss). After half an hour, I need a shower, and I'm not joking.

But visually, it's stunning. The setting changes with every level: a subway car, a Wild West town with cowboys on the balconies, a pirate ship, a *Matrix*-style lobby, and even levels plunged into darkness, where you locate enemies by sound.

The colours are bright, the animations are clean and everything stays readable in the middle of the chaos. When you clear a level in one go, it feels like you've just shot a movie scene in a single take.

## What about Batman?

![The Bat-Signal in the sky over Gotham in Batman: Arkham Shadow](/images/articles/quest3s-batman.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/batman-arkham-shadow/3551691271620960/)

Lots of people own *Batman: Arkham Shadow*, because Meta gave it away with Quest 3 and Quest 3S headsets bought [until the end of April 2025](https://www.roadtovr.com/quest-3-and-3s-batman-vr-bundle/). People often recommend it to me, but it doesn't appeal to me.

I don't like the character, or his universe. A billionaire dresses up and, at night, goes out to beat up Gotham's thugs, and that has never done anything for me.

He has the means to solve the problem, and yet the Joker escapes, again and again. He could fund the whole city's police force, but he prefers to buy gadgets. He has no powers, and he still tries to take on Superman.

Anyway, I don't understand the character at all.

Same goes for *Iron Man VR*. Tony Stark has the same profile, a billionaire fighting crime with gadgets, not very efficiently. Billionaire vigilantes are definitely not for me.

The ironic part is that *Arkham Shadow* and *Iron Man VR* both come from Camouflaj, the studio behind *République*. So my favourite game and the two games I refuse to launch were made by the same studio. My problem is with the heroes, the studio has nothing to do with it.

## What about Beat Saber?

![Two glowing sabers, one red and one blue, slicing cubes in Beat Saber](/images/articles/quest3s-beat-saber.webp)

→ [See it on the Meta Quest Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/beat-saber/2448060205267927/)

You may have noticed that the headset's most famous game is missing, *Beat Saber*, from Beat Games. It's a good first taste of virtual reality, with two sabers and cubes flying at you to the music, and within thirty seconds, you understand what the headset can do. But I don't think I've touched it in several months, you see everything it has to offer pretty quickly.
