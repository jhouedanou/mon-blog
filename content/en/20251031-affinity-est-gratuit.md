# Affinity Goes Free, and the Internet Is Already Upset

Affinity has, once again, just shaken things up. Until now, the creative suite was paid software, a one-time purchase for each major version, with no subscription, which made it a serious alternative to Adobe.
But recently, Affinity became free. Yes, free.
With one small condition: you now need a Canva account to access the AI-related features, such as image generation.

And honestly, that's excellent news.

## Why this is a good thing

I'm one of those who bought version 1, then version 2. And yet, I welcome this change.
Because a world where Adobe doesn't reign alone over graphic design is, in my view, a better world.

Let's be fair: Adobe remains a reference. I still haven't found an equivalent to Lightroom, particularly for editing the photos I take with my Ricoh Theta. Even Darktable or Affinity Photo don't quite reach that level.

But for everything else, Affinity remains my favourite choice thanks to its clear interface, more coherent than Adobe's, and its subscription-free business model. Add to that excellent performance and a unified experience across all platforms (except Linux, unfortunately, where there is still no native version).

The fact that this suite is becoming accessible to everyone, with no financial barrier, is great news for independent creatives.

## "Yes, but I paid for it"

It was bound to happen.

Naturally, not everyone agrees.

On one side, some users feel they should keep exclusive access since they bought their licence. On the other, some fear that the paid integration of Canva's AI features is the start of a transition to a subscription model.

And I understand those concerns.
But having tested this new version, let's call it Affinity 3.0, I can say it: this is a fine evolution.

## A unified, smooth, well-designed suite

The big news is that you no longer need to juggle three programs.
Everything is now gathered in a single application.

Three workspaces:

- "Vector" for vector drawing,
- "Layout" for page layout,
- "Pixel" for image editing.

It's a unified, smooth and very pleasant experience, even on my old MacBook Pros.
The shortcuts are unchanged, exports are simpler and faster, and the interface has gained in coherence.

Within a few minutes, I had found my bearings again.
No relearning, no frustration. Just the familiar feeling of working in a stable, efficient environment.

To tell the truth, to those who are outraged because they paid before everyone else, I don't have much to say. It's a bit like Maître Gims fans who claim to be the "real" ones because they listened to him chopping up instrumentals back in the Sexion d'Assaut days, and refuse to acknowledge his artistic evolution.

(Gims remains, whatever anyone says, an excellent lyrical singer. )

## In short

Affinity 3.0 is an excellent surprise.
A complete, modern, high-performance creative suite that is now accessible to everyone.
The AI tools are optional, the experience remains polished, and the original "anti-subscription" philosophy has not been betrayed.

So instead of taking offence, you might as well make the most of it.
Download it, try it, and above all, create.
