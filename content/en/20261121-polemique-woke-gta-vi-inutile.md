Let me be honest from the first line: GTA VI doesn't interest me. I told myself it would interest me the day Yango and Hostinger put on Vice City colours. That day came. The pink and purple are everywhere, right down to the app that finds me a driver and the dashboard that hosts my websites. Even my web host looks like it spent the summer in Miami.

That's the thing with GTA VI: even when you've decided to ignore it, it follows you. Like an ex who kept your Netflix password. The game came out on November 19, and two days later it had taken over my apps, my feeds, and YouTube had found its bone to chew on: GTA VI is supposedly "woke". That isn't a video game problem. It's a YouTube problem.

## What people hold against GTA VI

The critiques all sound alike, and one title sums them up well: "GTA 6 Is Simp Slop", a video by Locke The Bounty Hunter, one among dozens of the same kind. A *simp* is a man who is too submissive to a woman. *Slop* is cheap industrial mush. It's mean, and it's meant to be.

The complaint comes down to four points:

- **The gameplay.** Too many everyday chores, like fixing a sink or doing the shopping, and not enough car chases or heists.
- **Jason.** A protagonist with no charisma, at Lucia's service. People call it a "submissive boyfriend simulator".
- **Ideology.** The game is supposedly shaped by progressive ideas, with less freedom and satire that has lost its bite.
- **The verdict.** A guaranteed letdown for players of the older games.

Hours of videos to say that a 2026 game isn't San Andreas. Thanks, we noticed: the graphics have changed slightly. Let's go point by point.

## The real subject: YouTube lives on controversy

On YouTube, outrage is a business model. An angry title gets more clicks than a nuanced one. A thumbnail with a red arrow gets more clicks than a calm one. And the furious comments underneath count too: the algorithm reads them as engagement.

So a creator has no interest in saying "it's a good game with some debatable choices". Nobody shares that. The incentive is to find the angle that makes people angry. In 2026, that angle has a name: woke. It works on anything: a game, a film, a yoghurt ad.

I've already written that [a social network is not a grin](/en/20250605-facebook-nest-pas-un-grin), the street-corner tea circle where people actually talk: online, nobody discusses, everybody plays to the gallery. YouTube is the long-form version of the same problem. The gallery pays, in views.

## Jason and Lucia: a couple's dynamic, not submission

The most shared clip shows Jason buying flowers. Proof that he's submissive, apparently.

Let's put it back in context. Those flowers are part of a mission tied to Lucia getting out of prison. A man who greets his partner with flowers when she walks out of prison is a man who cares about her. In a game where you steal cars, it's almost the most subversive act in the whole script.

And the critics forget the other direction. When Jason gets shot, Lucia is the one who takes care of him.

He looks after her, she looks after him. Maintaining the relationship is part of the game, like maintaining your weapons or your car. That's called a healthy couple's dynamic. If that's being a *simp*, then half the couples married for thirty years are simps, and they seem to be coping fine.

## Cal and Jason in the same bed: context, again

Another scene taken out of context: Cal and Jason wake up in the same bed. Some people see it as proof that the game is pushing a gay relationship.

The context is simple. Cal visits Jason. They drink a bit too much. They wake up in the same bed. That's it.

Anyone who was twenty once and had a friend crash on a sofa that was too small knows the scene. I only know it by hearsay. At twenty-one, I had no friends. I lived in a studio across from the Université de Sherbrooke, too busy with my e-commerce classes to make any. No sofa too small, nobody to sleep on it, just me and case studies on abandoned shopping carts.

But I trust the people who had a social life: it's a morning-after gag, not a manifesto. Cutting the scene before and the scene after is manufacturing the controversy in the edit.

## "There's nothing to do": really?

The last complaint: you spend your time being the nice partner, with nothing to do outside the relationship.

Take the painting, held up as proof of chores. It isn't a task Lucia hands to Jason. He lives in a run-down house, and he fixes it up. Restoring your home is what people do when they want to stand on their own feet. If painting a wall makes you submissive, every dad on a Saturday morning is under someone's spell. For a character coming out of a life of petty schemes, it's actually a good piece of writing.

The game has its share of burglaries, carjackings and stupid activities. It's GTA. Doing the shopping between two heists doesn't remove the heists. It gives them a setting. Even robbers need toilet paper.

## The GTA quota is met

Personally, I think the usual specification is met: vulgarity, nudity, violence. It's all there. Rockstar ticked every box, and probably two or three the censors haven't invented yet.

And it sits on top of a somewhat tragic story. A mother who abandons her child for a heist that goes wrong. An ex-husband dealing drugs. This is far from a game sanitised by a committee of the right-thinking. It's a GTA that simply decided its characters would have a life between the shootouts.

The satire hasn't disappeared. It has changed target. It aims less at passers-by and more at the people who live in this world. You may not like that, but it isn't ideology. It's writing.

## My take: beautiful, and I won't play it

Visually, GTA VI is gorgeous. Vice City has never looked this believable. But it runs at 30 frames per second on PS5 and Xbox Series, and [Rockstar confirmed it](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/grand-theft-auto-6-will-run-at-only-30-fps-on-consoles-at-launch-no-performance-mode-promised-even-the-souped-up-ps5-pro-might-not-get-60-fps-support) without promising a 60 fps mode. For a driving and shooting game in 2026, you feel it.

Above all, it isn't a game I would play. Not because it's supposedly woke. Because my gaming evenings happen elsewhere, with a headset on, waving my arms around the living room like a man fighting invisible mosquitoes. The Quest 3S has better games for me, and I wrote [the list, from easiest to hardest](/en/20261008-jeux-quest-3s-recommandations).

So I don't hold anything against GTA VI. I hold it against these videos for selling anger wrapped up as a review. A game can disappoint. It can be slow, too scripted, locked at 30 fps. Those are real arguments. "Jason buys his girlfriend flowers" isn't one. As far as I know, it's the bare minimum.

One last thing. Before writing this post, I watched [the walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ve2M5i_DSKI&t=35727s), and I understand the hype. Vice City feels alive. The characters have a story. The missions are not just household chores. I also understand why the critics picked the flowers and the bed: across hours of gameplay, those are the only minutes that could make a scandal.

I still won't play it. But I have no illusions left: I can ignore GTA VI, it won't ignore me.
