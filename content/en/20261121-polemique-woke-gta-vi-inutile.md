Let me be honest from the first line: GTA VI doesn't interest me.

I told myself it would interest me the day Yango and Hostinger put on Vice City colours, and that day came. The pink and purple are everywhere, in the app that finds me a driver as much as in the dashboard that hosts my websites, and my web host now looks like it spent the summer in Miami.

The trouble with GTA VI is that it follows you once you've decided to ignore it, like an ex who kept your Netflix password. The game came out on November 19, and two days later it had taken over my apps and my feeds, while YouTube found its bone to chew on. GTA VI is supposedly "woke". And once again, YouTube is the one that deserves a closer look, not the game.

## What people hold against GTA VI

The critiques all sound alike, and one title sums them up well: "GTA 6 Is Simp Slop", a video by Locke The Bounty Hunter, one among dozens of the same kind, where a *simp* is a man too submissive to a woman and *slop* is cheap industrial mush. It's mean, and it's meant to be.

The complaint comes down to four points:

- **The gameplay.** Too many everyday chores, like fixing a sink or doing the shopping, and not enough car chases or heists.
- **Jason.** A protagonist with no charisma, at Lucia's service, to the point that people call it a "submissive boyfriend simulator".
- **Ideology.** The game is supposedly shaped by progressive ideas, with less freedom and satire that has lost its bite.
- **The verdict.** A guaranteed letdown for players of the older games.

That's hours of video to say that a 2026 game isn't San Andreas, which we had already noticed, if only from the graphics. Let's take it in order.

## YouTube lives on controversy

On YouTube, outrage is a business model: an angry title gets more clicks than a nuanced one, a thumbnail with a red arrow gets more than a calm one, and the furious comments underneath count too, since the algorithm reads them as engagement.

So a creator has no interest in saying "it's a good game with some debatable choices", because nobody shares that. The incentive is to find the angle that makes people angry, and in 2026 that angle is called woke. It works on anything, a game, a film or a yoghurt ad.

I've already written that [a social network is not a grin](/en/20250605-facebook-nest-pas-un-grin), the street-corner tea circle where people actually talk, because online nobody discusses, everybody plays to the gallery. YouTube is the long-form version, except that over there the gallery pays, in views.

## Jason, Lucia and the bunch of flowers

The most shared clip shows Jason buying flowers, proof that he's submissive, apparently.

In fact, those flowers are part of a mission tied to Lucia getting out of prison, and a man who greets his partner with flowers when she walks out of prison is a man who cares about her. In a game where you steal cars, it's almost the most subversive act in the whole script.

The critics also forget the other direction, since when Jason gets shot, Lucia is the one who takes care of him.

He looks after her, she looks after him, and maintaining the relationship is part of the game just like maintaining your weapons or your car. I call that a couple that works, and if that's being a *simp*, then half the couples married for thirty years are simps, and they seem to be coping fine.

## Cal and Jason in the same bed

Another scene taken out of context: Cal and Jason wake up in the same bed, and some people see it as proof that the game is pushing a gay relationship.

Cal visits Jason, they drink a bit too much, and they wake up in the same bed. That's it.

Anyone who was twenty once and had a friend crash on a sofa that was too small knows the scene. I only know it by hearsay, because at twenty-one I had no friends. I lived in a studio across from the Université de Sherbrooke, too busy with my e-commerce classes to make any, with no sofa too small and nobody to sleep on it, and only case studies on abandoned shopping carts for company.

So I trust the people who had a social life to recognise a morning-after gag. And when you cut the scene before and the scene after, you manufacture the controversy in the edit.

## "There's nothing to do"

The last complaint: you spend your time being the nice partner, with nothing to do outside the relationship.

Take the painting, held up as proof of chores. Lucia doesn't hand it to Jason. He lives in a run-down house and he fixes it up, like anyone who wants to stand on their own feet. If painting a wall makes you submissive, every dad is under someone's spell on Saturday mornings. For a character coming out of a life of petty schemes, I find that detail rather well written.

The game has its share of burglaries, carjackings and stupid activities, because it's GTA, and doing the shopping between two heists takes nothing away from the heists. Robbers need toilet paper too.

## No shooting in a gay club, no sex workers

The other complaint going around is that you supposedly can't open fire in a gay club, and that you can no longer pay for the strippers' services or hire a sex worker, a first for the series since 2001, [as IBTimes points out](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/gta-6-removes-sex-worker-mechanic-strip-clubs-remain-1833292). For some, that's proof the game was censored by wokeness.

But nobody asks why.

For the gay club, just look at the map. Leonida is Florida, and in 2016, in Orlando, a man killed 49 people at Pulse, a gay nightclub. Rockstar hasn't explained anything officially, but I can't see a studio letting players replay the worst anti-gay mass shooting in American history in the state where it happened. I call that common sense, and probably the opinion of a few lawyers.

For sex workers, the answer is even simpler. Since GTA III, everyone knew the mechanic. You pay, then you kill the woman to get your money back, and for twenty-five years it was the example dragged out in every debate about violence in video games. Rockstar finally closed the file, and nobody will miss that feature, except the people making videos to say they miss it.

And don't tell me the game has gone soft.

## The GTA quota is met

The usual specification is met, with vulgarity, nudity and violence all present, and two mechanics fewer don't make it a children's game.

It all sits on top of a somewhat tragic story, about a mother who abandons her child for a heist that goes wrong and an ex-husband dealing drugs. This is far from a game sanitised by a committee of the right-thinking. Rockstar simply decided its characters would have a life between the shootouts.

On one point, I do agree with the complainers, just not for the same reasons. Personally, apart from Lucia, I find the way this game portrays women disastrous. Lucia has a story, choices and anger, while the others are set dressing, bodies in bikinis, dancers and sidekicks, and the official poster is enough to see it. If anyone is looking for a real criticism of GTA VI, I'd suggest starting there rather than with a bunch of flowers.

As for the satire, it has changed target and aims less at passers-by than at the people who live in this world. You may not like that, but we're talking about a writing choice, not ideology.

## Beautiful, and I won't play it

Visually, GTA VI is gorgeous, and Vice City has never looked this believable. But it runs at 30 frames per second on PS5 and Xbox Series, and [Rockstar confirmed it](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/grand-theft-auto-6-will-run-at-only-30-fps-on-consoles-at-launch-no-performance-mode-promised-even-the-souped-up-ps5-pro-might-not-get-60-fps-support) without promising a 60 fps mode, which you feel in a driving and shooting game in 2026.

Above all, I wouldn't play it, and wokeness has nothing to do with it. I wouldn't play it because my gaming evenings happen elsewhere, with a headset on, waving my arms around the living room like a man fighting invisible mosquitoes. The Quest 3S has better games for me, and I wrote [the list, from easiest to hardest](/en/20261008-jeux-quest-3s-recommandations).

These videos have every right to criticise GTA VI. What bothers me is that they sell anger wrapped up as a review. A game can disappoint, be slow, too scripted or locked at 30 fps, and those are real arguments. "Jason buys his girlfriend flowers" isn't one. As far as I know, it's the bare minimum.

One last thing. Before writing this post, I watched [the walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ve2M5i_DSKI&t=35727s), and I understand the hype, because Vice City feels alive, the characters have a story and the missions are not just household chores. I also understand why the critics picked the flowers and the bed, since across hours of gameplay, those are the only minutes that could make a scandal.

I still won't play it. But I have no illusions left. I can ignore GTA VI, it won't ignore me.
