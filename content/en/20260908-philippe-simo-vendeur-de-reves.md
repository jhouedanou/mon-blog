Since early August, the Franco-African web has been piling onto Philippe Simo, because he said that at 30, if you don't own your main residence, "poverty is lying in wait for you". Dozens of channels, including those that have made "Philippe Simo bashing" their bread and butter, [got their audience for the week out of it](/en/20260822-oui-a-lexcision-non-diversion-rhetorique).

I'm not going to take part in that (I've already said what I think of [the opinion givers the internet handed a microphone to](/en/20241022-restons-zen)), so no attacks on the man, no "feymân", no trial for fraud. I'm going to talk about what he says and what it's worth.

## The line about owning a home at 30 is false

Saying that at 30 you're either an owner or poor is false in our context, and it needs to be said.

In Côte d'Ivoire as in Cameroon, mortgages are reserved for a minority. You need a permanent contract, a down payment, guarantees, and a bank willing to lend over fifteen years at a decent rate. Most young workers are in the informal sector or on short contracts. What holds them back is access to financing, far more than willpower.

His line also mixes two things up. Buying your main residence is not the same thing as building wealth. A plot of land, a business, training or seed capital can bring in more than a flat bought too early and too expensive.

There, criticism done, and it fits in a few lines.

## Dream seller is not an insult

People use the term as an accusation, but I take it literally.

A dream seller is someone who shows a life you didn't allow yourself to imagine. In a region where you learn very early to aim small, that job has real value.

And his dream is achievable. He isn't talking about a lottery, miracle trading or "become a millionaire in three months", he says to save part of what you earn, invest back home, build wealth over ten years and stop putting your money into things that lose value. It's slow, it's boring and it takes discipline, but it's doable on a normal salary.

That's also why his line hurt so much. Many of those insulting him don't have an income problem, they've been earning a decent living for ten years and have put nothing aside. The message was aimed straight at them, and being shaken up from time to time does no harm.

## We need more people like him

Our public role models are footballers, artists, and a fair number of people whose money comes from nobody knows where (I'm not naming names, you know them). A guy who talks about saving, investing back home and building wealth on channels followed by hundreds of thousands of people pulls us upward, even when he fumbles a line.

The problem is that he's almost alone in talking about this. If there were twenty Philippe Simos with different approaches, a botched statement about turning 30 would be one opinion among others and wouldn't turn into [a manhunt](/en/20250115-l-histoire-danne).

So, for those who want to move forward, debate the idea, correct what's wrong, but don't wreck one of the only useful conversations we've had this year.

For the others, you can go back to your stories about "resting" 🙂
