# Why so much hatred for Adobe? Because it's deserved.

Although Affinity Designer is a fine alternative, in a professional setting I have no other choice but to use Adobe. Most of the designs I have to turn into code and websites are made with Adobe, and I don't yet know Affinity Photo 2 well enough to confidently edit the RAW files coming out of my Theta Z1 camera.

So I have to use Adobe... but sometimes at the expense of my mental health, not to mention the price. I'll admit my judgement may be a little exaggerated, but I am deeply disappointed to have no alternative to Adobe.

Well, this morning my frustration reached its peak. I HATE Adobe, and I've sworn to dive headfirst into the Affinity suite, far from Adobe's hassles. Let me explain.

It all started on Friday the 13th of December, when I wanted to update the payment methods on an Adobe account. First surprise in a series that made the start of that day particularly hard:

- You can't do it yourself, because the menu command that lets you do it everywhere else simply doesn't exist. You have to go through their customer service, which is anything but quick and simple.
- After clicking the "help" button, you're faced with an artificial intelligence that seems designed either to test your patience or to make you give up.

Unluckily for them, I'm the patient and determined type when it comes to certain companies (including, but not limited to, my bank, Apple and Adobe). And a nice guy too, so here's a 3-step tutorial for escaping the AI and reaching a human:

1. **Demand a human**: insist, repeat "I want to talk to a human" until the AI gives in (while staying polite).
2. **Pick an option, any option**: no need to be precise, what matters is moving forward.
3. **Persist**: keep going until a human agent finally deigns to step in.

After a lot of effort, I managed to talk to an advisor. Not without reluctance, he granted my request. Everything finally seemed settled, until I discovered, 30 minutes later, an Adobe Express subscription added to my account.

I don't even use that service and I have no wish to pay more than necessary. What's more, Canva is far better when it comes to all-in-one, easy-to-pick-up editors.

A new battle with customer service followed: "I don't want Adobe Express, I don't use it and I don't want to pay for it." After going through two customer advisors, I cancelled that unsolicited subscription. But the experience left a bitter taste: Adobe, through its abusive practices, draws criticism that is largely deserved, along with all the hatred in the world. Online, in real life and in the courts.

Because yes, Adobe is facing several lawsuits over questionable business practices:

- **Hidden cancellation fees**: the US Department of Justice is suing Adobe for concealing cancellation fees that can reach several hundred dollars. These fees apply to annual subscriptions paid monthly.
- **Misleading interfaces**: the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) accuses Adobe of designing interfaces that push users to subscribe without clearly disclosing the terms. Cancelling then becomes an obstacle course.

These stories reveal a well-oiled strategy for making the user experience deliberately frustrating.

In conclusion: stop using their services, if you can. Or go for a monthly subscription with no commitment (i.e. Affinity).
