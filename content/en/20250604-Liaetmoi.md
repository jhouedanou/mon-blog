# A year and more with artificial intelligence: productivity gains, maths homework and the risk of mental laziness
<iframe width="100%" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/-NsSPJ8dTfI" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen></iframe>


After more than a year of near-daily use of artificial intelligence, I felt like sharing my experience.

Here are the pros and cons I've noticed, somewhere between surprises, struggles, and small everyday victories.

## AI and my niece's maths homework

My niece has just passed her CEPE with 137 points out of 170, even though, like her uncle at her age (and well after), she has serious trouble with maths.

This is where ChatGPT comes in.

Thanks to its voice mode, it often explains things better than I do (I admit it without shame). For example, I had forgotten how to do long division or check a calculation by casting out nines... The AI, on the other hand, handles it very well.

So we're thinking of getting her a ChatGPT subscription for her first year of secondary school, because at that stage maths gets tougher, and having a patient, clear assistant available 24/7 is a real plus.

## Coding with AI is a pleasure

For a long time, I resisted. GitHub Copilot, WindSurf, Cursor... not for me. I code mainly in PHP, a bit in JavaScript and Vue.js, and React and Angular never really won me over.

I was afraid AI would make me a worse developer, hand me ready-made solutions and push me even further away from the subtleties of languages I only imperfectly master.

And then I discovered Claude.

By using it thoughtfully, I understood that AI could become a real ally. Result: I code faster, I debug better, and I find solutions I wouldn't have thought of on my own.

For example, I recently built a site in Vue.js, with scroll-linked GSAP animations. Before, it would have taken me 2 weeks. This time, it was wrapped up in 3 days.

I also used Cursor to improve [Project Fat Loss](https://jhouedanou.github.io/projectfatloss/), my fitness tracking app, a little. Cursor helped me optimise the code, fix bugs, and even add features I hadn't considered.

But beware: AI is not (yet) autonomous. It needs our vigilance. Claude and Cursor, for example, can sometimes get things badly wrong.

One day, in a burst of unapologetic laziness (it was a Sunday, after all), I asked it to fix a timing issue in an animation while I went off to do a bit of exercise. When I came back... the scroll no longer worked, the animations were flying all over the place, and a good chunk of my code had been rewritten.

Since then, I've learned my lesson and no longer delegate fixes to it unsupervised. In the same spirit, I don't really use AI in "agent" mode to code. I prefer to ask it precise questions, ask it to help me find solutions, or to suggest improvements.

That lets me keep control over my code while still benefiting from the valuable help it can provide (not to mention that it helps me better understand the proposed solutions and learn at the same time).

## AI and sending emails

Honestly, I can't remember the last time I wrote an email without help. For my professional as well as personal exchanges, AI saves me a huge amount of time.

It rephrases, corrects, clarifies my ideas, and drastically reduces spelling mistakes.

I mainly use the writing tools in Google Workspace, most likely powered by Gemini. They're particularly effective for long or complex messages.

Of course, it can smooth out the personal touch a bit, but in return, the messages are clear, precise and well structured.

## And what about video?

I don't know, I haven't tried it and I'm not sure I want to. But I'm curious to see how AI could help me in that area in the future.

To be honest, I may be one of the few people who find the evolution of video-generating AI a little horrifying. There is something troubling about this ability to create visual content so realistic that it becomes hard to tell the real from the fake.

Between increasingly convincing deepfakes and the possibility of generating any scene imaginable, we're entering territory where the line between reality and fiction blurs dangerously.

## But beware of mental laziness (i.e. assisted writing)

AI is shaking up our habits, optimising our work, and can even help us learn better. It's fast, accessible, and often stunningly effective.

But it can also make us lazy. We tend to delegate too many tasks to it, and sometimes we forget to think for ourselves. It's a bit like leaving our brain in standby mode.

I recently caught myself writing a fairly inspired text, spontaneously, then whipping out Gemini on my phone to "clean it up", smooth it out, improve it. In itself, nothing wrong with that, it's even handy. Especially since, at the time, I had to reply to some frankly tiresome colleagues: a clear, concise, well-turned text with no unnecessary emotion can make all the difference.

But looking back, I asked myself: at what point did I stop making the effort myself for this kind of task? Am I not losing my spontaneity?

Since then, I've stopped doing that. I prefer to write my drafts by hand, or at least without AI, to keep that creative spark intact. And above all, so as not to lose the ability to think, create and learn on my own.
