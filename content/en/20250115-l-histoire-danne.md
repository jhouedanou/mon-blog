_In other words, we can talk about it with peace of mind, without exploiting the distress and despair of the victims._

My use of social media is limited. I mostly stick to YouTube, which I listen to in the background while doing other things, and a few accounts meant to relay content from my blogs.

Yet the social network X (formerly Twitter) continues to intrigue me. That digital world seems to run by its own rules, where anything goes, where anything can happen.

A recent example perfectly illustrates its excesses: the Anne affair. A woman, victim of a "brouteur" posing as Brad Pitt, who extorted a colossal 830,000 euros from her in a year and a half. After a news report told her story, Twitter was quickly overrun with mockery. Within a few hours, what should have remained a private matter became a public spectacle.

Clips from the report, often taken out of context, went around. The most striking passages were turned into memes and fed an escalation of jokes and parodies. The "Brad Pitt" hashtag went viral, fuelled by mocking comments and sometimes cruel parodies. [The phenomenon feeds on itself](/en/20260822-oui-a-lexcision-non-diversion-rhetorique), each person trying to outdo the others with their "contribution".

But behind the laughter lies a more complex story.

## How Anne fell for the fake Brad Pitt scam

Even before this affair, Anne was already going through hard times. She was fighting brain cancer, was living with HIV and had just been through a gruelling divorce. In 2023, she discovers social media, a world where she is still a novice, unaware of the many traps hidden there: manipulated images, identity theft, [deep fakes](/en/20250604-liaetmoi), and so on.

It all starts with a harmless message from a woman claiming to be Brad Pitt's mother. This person builds a relationship of trust with Anne, writing to her regularly. Gradually, other characters are introduced into the story: Brad Pitt himself, his agent, his manager. The scammers are nothing if not ingenious. They fabricate fake press articles, doctored videos, official documents and even fake passports. They use [American phone numbers](/en/20260831-faux-mail-renouvellement-zoho-358-dollars) to reinforce the illusion.

The trap closes when the fake Brad Pitt announces he is suffering from kidney cancer. Anne, moved by the announcement, starts responding to the financial requests sent her way. In a year and a half, she transfers a total of 830,000 euros, convinced she is helping a celebrity in distress.

## After the scam, the online lynching

The affair left Anne with nothing. Today, she lives in a 6 m² storage unit, surrounded by a few boxes. She has attempted suicide several times and is now under psychiatric treatment. What is even more striking is the impact of the online lynching she is going through. Social media, instead of condemning the scam, focuses on her, piling on the mockery and the parodies.

To put it more plainly: Anne has become X's new "punchline" or "punching bag". The situation has been amplified by the fact that most people don't know Anne. They don't know what she went through, or what she did to survive, or that she is living with HIV and has already attempted suicide three times.

We all make mistakes, but we all deserve a minimum of respect. Above all, **there are times when silence is golden: if you're not sure that what you're about to post will do any good, keep scrolling.**
