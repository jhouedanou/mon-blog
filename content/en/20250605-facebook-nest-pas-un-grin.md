# When a Community Manager Mistakes Social Media for the "Grin"

**Hello.**

I'm Jean-Luc Houédanou, web developer and unapologetic defender of common sense.

Thinking rigorously, respecting others, weighing your words: these are qualities you expect from a professional, especially in a field as symbolic as books and communication. I'm not here to lecture anyone, only to remind people that social media is not a *grin*, that informal group of friends where you argue about everything and nothing, often without substance or filter.

Yet sometimes you get the feeling that certain professionals have forgotten these basics.

## A dismaying step backwards

Recently, the community manager of a well-known player in the Ivorian book trade thought it wise to comment on how well women master the French language, in a manner that was, to put it mildly... clumsy.

His "solution": hand out a Bescherelle grammar guide, with a dash of sarcasm thrown in.

![Sexist comment from the community manager about women and iPhones](/images/articles/bescherelle/01.webp)

A reaction that, beyond the open contempt, shows an unfortunate mix of arrogance and shaky grammar.

And that is where it all falls apart.

## If you want to correct people, you'd better be flawless

A concrete example: « Ce qu'elles nous servent là est affreux » (roughly, "what they are serving us here is dreadful").

That turn of phrase, on top of being condescending, rests on a rather loose use of the verb "servir".

You don't "serve" a grammar mistake the way you serve a coffee. This awkward wording, used precisely to criticise other people's French, betrays an imperfect command of the language, which is quite something coming from someone who presumes to hand out Bescherelles.

Then there's this.

> « Allure de princesses, démarche de déesses, tête d'illettrés. Dehors ! Partagez au max, elles doivent voir. » ("Looks of princesses, gait of goddesses, heads of illiterates. Out! Share as much as you can, they need to see this.")

At first glance, you can feel the urge to "coin a punchy line". Look closer, though, and you notice that style and grammar don't always follow the intention.
A few observations:


- "Tête d'illettrés": here the word illettrés is in the masculine plural, while the subject of the sentence is feminine ("elles"). The correct form would therefore be tête d'illettrées.

- There is also a problem of stylistic coherence: "allure", "démarche" and "tête" are lined up in the same structure, but with images from different registers, which makes the whole thing wobble.


What was meant as a critique of other people's French thus turns against its author: you cannot correct others while forgetting basic agreement rules. Above all, it is never appropriate to pass such a sweeping judgement, let alone in a contemptuous tone, when you're addressing a wide and potentially young audience from an institutional page.

## The irony of the critic's stance

What we're witnessing here is an unfortunate drift: the role of the community manager, who is supposed to promote an institution and carry cultural messages, turns into a space for settling personal scores, tinged with sexist clichés and reckless judgements.

Next logical step: ban women from libraries on Friday evenings, or only let them into the "romance" and "self-help" shelves on the right for "the girls who dream", with political essays and *The 48 Laws of Power* on the left for "the real thinkers", the alpha males.

## A call to do better

This is not a trial, but a call for professionalism and respect.

It needs to be said: it was not the bookshop as a whole that spoke out. In fact, the bookshop issued a firm clarification, which I applaud.
![The bookshop's clarification](/images/articles/bescherelle/00.webp)

It was just one CM, poorly trained or poorly supervised, who in a few clicks turned a cultural showcase into a stage for misogynistic stand-up.

In conclusion, ladies, be yourselves.

That's precisely what bothers some people, and that's a good thing.
