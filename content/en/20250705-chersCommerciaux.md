Today I want to talk about something that concerns a lot of professionals in Abidjan: company-provided equipment. These tools are there for work, but they also record a good part of what you do.

## What people forget about company equipment

In Abidjan, many professionals are equipped with cutting-edge devices, but a lot of them seem to forget what these tools record. Your iPhone, MacBook or company car **observe, record and archive almost everything you do**.

The classic case is an employee using his work iPhone for nights out in Yopougon, his MacBook for personal projects, or his company car for a weekend in Assinie, all while thinking nobody knows. That's a mistake that can cost you dearly.

## Devices that are also sensors

Whether you're a sales rep, a developer or a community manager, your employer equips you to make your work more efficient, with a brand new iPhone, a lightning-fast MacBook and sometimes a company car.

This trio is a good productivity kit, but it's also a **network of silent sensors**, since these devices log your trips, your connections and your habits. And even when it's locked, an iPhone can still answer an NFC terminal, as the [Apple Pay Visa hack in Express Transit mode](/en/20260417-apple-pay-visa-faille-tap-to-pay) showed.

And yes, the car too, especially if an AirTag has been discreetly placed in it "for security". Officially, it's there to protect the vehicle, but it can also be used to keep an eye on your every detour. It won't stay discreet forever, though, because an AirTag separated from its owner that is moving with you eventually triggers the "AirTag Found Moving With You" alert on an iPhone, and phones running Android 6.0 or later also warn you automatically ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119874){target="_blank" rel="noopener"}).

## The dashboard example

One morning, your boss opens a dashboard and he **sees** your trips across Abidjan, the repeated stops in neighbourhoods with no connection to your clients, or the unplanned breaks. And one question comes up: "Why that detour to Cocody?"

Technically, it relies on three things:

* **AirTag + Find My**: these technologies allow [near real-time location](/en/20260730-telephone-perdu-retrouve-abobo-findhub), relayed by the many iPhones nearby.
* **iCloud**: photos, documents and locations can be synced automatically.
* **Timestamped history**: every detour and every stop is recorded, dated and geolocated.

And that's where things can go wrong, because your boss will ask you for explanations, and that can lead to suspicion or sanctions, when you thought you were enjoying a long weekend.

## What Ivorian law says about employee location tracking

**Law No. 2013-450** on data protection and the **ARTCI** (Côte d'Ivoire's Telecommunications/ICT Regulatory Authority) govern the monitoring of company property.

**For employers,** it is necessary to:
* Clearly inform employees about monitoring systems.
* Declare data processing to the competent authorities.
* Stay proportionate: the goal is to protect property, not to spy.

**For employees,** you have the right to be informed about monitoring, to access data about you, and to challenge any abusive use.

On the other hand, the law will not protect you if you use company equipment for personal rather than professional purposes.

## How to adopt the right approach?

There is no miracle solution, but an approach based on caution and transparency can save you a lot of trouble.

**As an employee:**
* **Understand what's at stake**: consider that every work tool can keep a record of what you do.
* **Separate work from personal life**: [never mix your uses](/en/20261028-whatsapp-nest-pas-un-moyen-de-communication-professionnel).
* **Ask questions**: don't hesitate to find out about the apps and technologies installed on your equipment.

**As an employer:**
* **Be transparent**: communicate clearly about monitoring tools.
* **Respect the law**: make sure your practices comply with Ivorian legislation.
* **Establish a clear charter**: define usage rules to avoid misunderstandings.

## It's up to you

An AirTag in your car, an iPhone in your pocket, a MacBook in your bag... They all keep a record of where you go, and those records had better match your job description.

Technology only documents, and it's up to you to decide whether that documentation will work for you or against you.
