iPadOS 26: The iPad Is (Finally) Useful Again

My iPad Mini had been gathering dust for a while. I used it for two things: taking handwritten notes in meetings (with a Ugreen Bluetooth stylus, good enough to avoid buying an overpriced Apple Pencil) and watching a few series. 

That's it.  I had tried to turn it into a backup work machine, but the experience was frustrating. Impossible, for instance, to follow a YouTube video while taking notes properly. "Split View" multitasking is rigid, limiting, and a long way from what a regular desktop environment offers.

## Then came iPadOS 19, or rather 26.

We can skip past the "Liquid Glass" transparency effects and the promise of an omnipresent AI (Apple Intelligence). The real change **is the arrival of floating windows**.
Yes, real windows. 
Resizable, movable, with proper workspace management. For the first time, you can work in several apps at once without feeling like you have to compromise.

The result: I can now follow a Udemy course on one side while retouching a photo in Affinity Photo on the other, without juggling or sacrificing readability.

![iPad mini 6](/images/articles/ipad-mini-6.webp )

## Does this turn the iPad into a computer? 

Not quite. For web development or heavy video/audio production, a MacBook (or a PC) remains essential.
But for note-taking, office work, light retouching or online training, the iPad becomes genuinely functional.

One small gripe in passing: the Liquid Glass visual effects sometimes hurt the readability of notifications. Nothing blocking, but worth mentioning.

In short, iPadOS 26 doesn't revolutionise everything, but it finally unlocks the iPad's real potential. And that's already a big step.
