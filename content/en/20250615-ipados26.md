Before iPadOS 26, my iPad mini had been gathering dust for a while. I used it for two things: taking handwritten notes in meetings (with a Ugreen Bluetooth stylus, good enough to avoid buying an overpriced Apple Pencil) and watching a few shows. 

That's it. I had tried to turn it into a backup work machine, but the experience was frustrating. You couldn't, for instance, follow a YouTube video while taking proper notes. "Split View" multitasking is rigid and a long way from what a regular desktop environment offers.

## Then came iPadOS 19, or rather 26

We can skip past the "Liquid Glass" transparency effects and the promise of an omnipresent AI ([Apple Intelligence](/en/20260326-lasagessedugeant)). The real change **is the arrival of floating windows**.
Yes, real windows. 
Resizable, movable, with proper workspace management. For the first time, you can work in several apps at once without feeling like you have to compromise.

The result: I can now follow a Udemy course on one side while retouching a photo in [Affinity Photo](/en/20251031-affinity-est-gratuit) on the other, without juggling or sacrificing readability.

![iPad mini 6 screen running iPadOS 26: the floating Affinity Photo window on top of an online course and a browser open on Google](/images/articles/ipad-mini-6.webp)

## Does iPadOS 26 turn the iPad mini into a computer?

Not quite. For web development or heavy video/audio production, [a MacBook (or a PC)](/en/20261008-pas-besoin-detre-dev-ou-barbu-mac-linux) remains essential.
But for note-taking, office work, light retouching or online training, the iPad becomes genuinely functional.

One small gripe in passing: the Liquid Glass visual effects sometimes hurt the readability of notifications. Not a dealbreaker, but worth mentioning.

In short, iPadOS 26 doesn't revolutionise everything, but it finally makes the iPad useful for work.
